Gaussian Splatting's Leap to E-commerce: We Finally Have a Standard

Until very recently, implementing Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) on a website was a headache. Yes, the visual quality was mind-blowing, but the technical ecosystem was a mess: every viewer used a proprietary format, and building a scalable production pipeline for e-commerce seemed almost impossible.

Joel Bastone Lopez 3D CGI Artist & Real-time WebGL Developer

But 2026 brought the news we were waiting for: the Khronos Group has ratified the official KHR_gaussian_splatting extension for the glTF format.

What does this mean in practice? Splats are now an official industry standard.

Pioneers in 3D Research

It's worth taking a pause to acknowledge that this leap didn't happen overnight. There are referents and artists who have been researching, teaching masterclasses, and spreading this technology long before it went "mainstream".

Personally, a big part of this technical vision comes from my former professor, Diego Viegas (known in the industry as Diego EV). He has been driving the use of advanced 3D and volumetric scanning in the Hispanic community for a long time. If you want to understand the workflow from scratch and generate captures on your own, I highly recommend watching his video tutorial on Gaussian Splatting on his YouTube channel.

The End of Format Chaos

For brands and agencies, relying on closed formats is a risk no one wants to take. The ratification of the glTF standard for 3DGS means you can now package a photorealistic product capture as easily as you export a JPG.

The main WebGL libraries have already taken note. Three.js integrated official native support in August of this year, and Babylon.js did so a few months earlier. The web workflow has finally been unified and democratized.

Why E-commerce Needs This NOW

Traditional photography has a limit to its visual impact, and classic 3D modeling (polygonal) usually struggles when it comes to complex materials like transparencies, reflective plastics, liquids, or furry organic textures—unless you use Ray Tracing (which is unfeasible in real-time on the web without frying the user's phone battery).

Gaussian Splatting solves this by replacing geometry with the rendering of millions of semi-transparent ellipses generated from real photos. The final result is brutal:

  • Absolute photorealism: It captures light, exact view-dependent reflections, and textures exactly as they are in the real world.
  • Extreme web performance: It runs at 60-120 frames per second directly in the mobile browser using WebGL and WebGPU. No external apps required.

A Real and Mature Production Pipeline

Giant platforms, like Zillow in the real estate sector, have already proven this year that 3DGS scales to enterprise-level production.

You can check out an interactive example at this link: www.zillow.com/showcase/demo.

Moreover, cloud tools like SuperSplat (by PlayCanvas) now allow you to clean, crop, and edit these point cloud scenes directly in the browser in a super intuitive way.

We have officially moved from the "technological experiment" stage to the "massive commercial production" stage.

If your online store is still showcasing flagship products with a flat gallery of 4 photos and a compressed video, you are giving away the wow factor to your competition. The integration of hyper-realistic interactive 3D is no longer a technical luxury; it's the new standard that skyrockets conversions. And now that files finally have a universal format, it's the exact moment to make the leap.
